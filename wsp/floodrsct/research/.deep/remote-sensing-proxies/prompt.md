Research question: What remote sensing and modeled flood data products could serve as alternatives or complements to NFIP claims for measuring flood damage/extent at the ZCTA level in the United States?

Context: We are building a geospatial flood damage prediction framework (FloodRSCT). NFIP claims have severe biases (penetration, cap, fraud, private market growth). We want to know what satellite, model-based, or hybrid data products exist that could provide flood extent, depth, or damage estimates independently of insurance data.

Sub-questions to investigate:
1. Sentinel-1 SAR flood mapping (RAPID, Copernicus EMS) -- resolution, temporal coverage, latency, access
2. NOAA/NWS National Water Model (NWM) Retrospective -- resolution, temporal span, what it actually outputs, access
3. First Street Foundation flood model -- coverage, resolution, access restrictions, cost
4. MODIS/VIIRS Near Real-Time flood products -- resolution, coverage, limitations
5. Landsat/Sentinel-2 optical flood mapping -- cloud limitations, temporal gaps
6. FEMA Hazus model outputs -- what's publicly available vs what requires local runs
7. Any emerging products (Planet, commercial SAR, AI-based flood mapping)

For each source, assess: spatial resolution, temporal coverage, access method/cost, known limitations, and suitability as a ZCTA-level flood damage proxy. Rank them by practical utility for our use case.

Source hierarchy: Government reports > peer-reviewed papers > court filings > research institutions > investigative journalism. Avoid blog posts and SEO aggregators.

Be skeptical. Distinguish documented facts from expert opinion. Never cite a source you haven't read.
